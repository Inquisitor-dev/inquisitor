Looking down at a steep angle at a huge heap piled up in the cramped back storeroom of a video arcade. The heap fills the entire frame from edge to edge; the edges of the image cut through it. No open floor, no walls, no door, no ceiling, no lamp, no person. Because the heap is so big, every object in it looks small. Bright, even light covers the whole heap: no dark corners, every object is clearly visible.

For twenty years the owner has thrown everything into this room and never sorted it: spare parts from broken machines, repair tools, things kids forgot on the machines, leftover party supplies, snacks, old toys and gadgets. The things have piled up into a tall chaotic mound, but everything obeys gravity: things rest on top of each other, some have tipped over, half fallen out of torn boxes and crates, small things have slid into the gaps between bigger ones. Nothing floats, no object passes through another. Most objects are only partly visible. Not a bit of floor is visible anywhere. More than one hundred and fifty objects in total.
